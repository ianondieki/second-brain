# P19-A · Tracker side states: INFO_REQUESTED, EXPIRED, ON_HOLD (REQ-ENG-10 part; AC-PROP-3; AC-TRACK-4 part)

Agent: impl-backend (xhigh; `engagements/` is security-sensitive). Worktree. Depends on P19-M (`engagement_notes`).
Map: scratchpad `p19/map-tracker.md`. Spec: `docs/spec/06` §6.9 side branches, `policy.yaml` `expire_bd`.

## Scope

The DB already knows the states (enums, CHECKs, the chain trigger's "return to the state you left" rule, system
events with `actor_role='system'`). The transition table, commands, deadlines, the expiry job and notifications do not.

1. **`INFO_REQUESTED`.** `Command.REQUEST_INFO` by ORG `DECIDERS` from `SUBMITTED` and `UNDER_REVIEW`, with a body
   `{lock_version, question: str (1–2000)}`; target `INFO_REQUESTED`; the stage clock pauses: the event's
   `stage_deadline_at` is null. `Command.ANSWER_INFO` by the developer from `INFO_REQUESTED`, body `{lock_version,
   answer}`; returns to the state it was entered from (the chain trigger enforces it) with `stage_deadline_at` =
   the deadline that state had + the paused business days (compute from the entering event's `stage_deadline_at`
   and the pause length in Kenyan business days; `calendar.add_business_days`). Both write an `engagement_notes` row
   (`info_request`, `info_answer`) with the event's `seq`. Notices: N03 to the developer on the request, N01-style to
   the organisation on the answer (see 4). `whose_turn`: the developer while paused. `pending()`: one item for the
   developer ("Answer the organisation's question"). `available()`: the organisation cannot decline or approve while
   paused (409 `illegal_transition`), it waits for the answer.
2. **`ON_HOLD` before the agreement.** `Command.PAUSE` by either party (ORG `DECIDERS`, the developer) from any
   `BEFORE_AGREEMENT` state that is not `SUBMITTED` nor a side state, body `{lock_version, reason: 1–500 chars,
   resume_at: date}` with `resume_at` after today and at most `policy.on_hold.max_days` (60) later; target `ON_HOLD`;
   `stage_deadline_at` = end of `resume_at` (so `due` shows the resume date). `Command.RESUME` by either party, or
   by the system at `resume_at`; returns to the entered-from state with its deadline shifted by the hold's length in
   business days. Notes `hold` and `resume`. After `AGREEMENT_SIGNING` the command is refused (409): the
   acknowledgement-or-dispute branch is the next phase (record on the card, not in the API as a half feature).
3. **`EXPIRED` by the system.** A periodic job `jobs/expiry.py` (`@app.periodic` every 15 minutes, like reminders;
   register in `jobs/app.py` IMPORT_PATHS) that, on the shared clock, finds engagements past `expire_bd` of their
   state and appends a system event (`actor_role='system'`, no user, the job bound to the acting party as
   `_ACTING_PARTY` does): `SUBMITTED` → `EXPIRED (NO_REVIEW)` at 20 BD, `UNDER_REVIEW` → `NO_DECISION` at 30 BD,
   `INTEREST_CONFIRMED` → `CONTACT_NOT_MADE` at 10 BD, `ORG_INTEREST` → `NO_DEV_RESPONSE` at 5 BD; the same job
   resumes `ON_HOLD` engagements whose `resume_at` has passed. The tag's `status` becomes `expired` (projection,
   AC-PROP-3). `expire_bd` counts from `stage_entered_at` and is paused by `INFO_REQUESTED` (the shifted deadline
   carries it: expire at `stage_deadline_at + (expire_bd − due_bd)` business days, computed once per engagement).
   Idempotent, one engagement per transaction with the FOR UPDATE lock, never a terminal state twice.
4. **Notifications.** `notify.compose()` returns None for system events today; make expiry and auto-resume notify
   both parties in-app (kinds `engagement.n01`, `n03`, `n05`, `n17` as the matrix rows say; `DECLINE_LABELS`-style
   sentences with the reason in words) and by email through a small branded template (`simple.html.j2`, one
   sentence, one link to the tracker; EM6's full form stays REQ-NOT-05). Requests, answers, pauses and resumes
   notify the other party in-app and by email the same way (N03, N20).
5. **Reminders.** `reminders/health.py` and the digests treat `INFO_REQUESTED` and `ON_HOLD` as paused: no "overdue",
   the render already has the labels (render.py 54-58, 185).
6. **Policy.** `policy.yaml`: `on_hold: {max_days: 60}`; `policy.py`'s strict loader learns the section; `expire_bd`
   is read for the four states above.
7. **API.** `router.py`: `POST /api/engagements/{id}/request-info`, `/answer-info`, `/pause`, `/resume` (bodies in
   `schemas.py`); `EngagementDetail` gains `notes: list[NoteOut]` (kind, body, resume_at, by: party, at) in event
   order and `paused_from: EngagementState | None` (the state a side state will return to) so the stepper can keep
   the stage marked. `make openapi api-types`.

## Decisions taken (impl-backend, 2026-10-02)

The card left these open; each is the thinnest honest option.

1. **Resume by a party carries a reason** (`{lock_version, reason: 1–500, one line}`), stored as the `resume` note
   (revision 0006: "the reason of an early resume"). The system's resume at the date writes no note (system events
   take none). Questions and answers are 1–2000 characters (the note's CHECK), reasons 1–500; text is trimmed, blank
   is 422 `invalid_note`.
2. **A hold's dates.** `resume_at` is the Africa/Nairobi date it resumes: after today, at most `on_hold.max_days`
   (60) calendar days ahead (else 422 `invalid_resume_at`). The hold's `stage_deadline_at` is the end of that date, so
   `due` reads it; the job resumes it from 00:00 EAT that day. The business days paused are those after the pause's
   Nairobi date up to the resume's (`calendar.business_days_between`); a same-day pause and resume moves nothing.
3. **Where the paused deadline lives.** Entering `INFO_REQUESTED` or `ON_HOLD` writes `paused_due_on` (the deadline's
   date; deadlines are end-of-day) and, for a hold, `resume_at` into the event's payload (dates only, in the hash
   chain); the return sets `paused_due_on` + the business days paused. Texts live only in notes, never in payloads,
   logs, audit details or notifications.
4. **Expiry is counted from the stage's entry from the main path**, plus the business days its deadline was moved by
   pauses, and happens once the end of the `expire_bd`-th business day has passed. For ORG_INTEREST, SUBMITTED and
   UNDER_REVIEW this equals the card's `stage_deadline_at + (expire_bd − due_bd)`; for INTEREST_CONFIRMED the
   deadline is the named contact-by date (up to 5 BD), so the card's formula would expire it as early as +5 BD,
   against REQ-ENG-05 / AC-MAIL-2 ("+10 BD from entering 3"). `policy.py` now requires `expire_bd` on exactly those
   four stages and refuses it before `due_bd`.
5. **How the job finds engagements.** It asks `app_engagements_due_for_expiry(now)` (revision 0007: ids only, a
   superset of what is due, called with no user bound) and decides each listed engagement again in a session bound
   to its developer (revision 0003's acting-party rule), one per transaction under `FOR UPDATE`. Each engagement's
   work, its session included, runs in its own try: a failure is logged, reported and retried by the next run.
   `user_ids` restricts a run (tests: other tests' engagements share the database). An expired tagged engagement's
   tag closes as `expired` (`app_close_tag(tag, 'expired')`, revision 0007); every other ending keeps the
   one-argument call.
6. **Endorsements survive a pause.** The database counts a return from a side state as a new `stage_round`; the
   facts and the detail's endorsement rows count from the round of the stage's latest entry from the main path, so a
   hold at CONTACT_MADE does not lose the organisation's endorsement of first contact (it would deadlock otherwise).
7. **While paused.** INFO_REQUESTED: the developer answers (whose turn) or withdraws; the organisation cannot
   approve or decline (409) but may withdraw its own question (`cancel_request`). ON_HOLD: nobody's turn; either
   party resumes (organisation deciders), the developer may withdraw. `WITHDRAW` starts from a paused state only
   when the stage it returns to is before the agreement. The named contact keeps the developer's contact details
   during a hold or question (the stage it returns to decides).
8. **API shape.** `paused_from` is on `EngagementSummary` (so lists and the detail carry it); `notes` on
   `EngagementDetail` only. Routes: `POST /api/engagements/{id}/request-info {lock_version, question}`,
   `/answer-info {lock_version, answer}`, `/cancel-request {lock_version}`, `/pause {lock_version, reason,
   resume_at}`, `/resume {lock_version, reason}`; each returns `EngagementDetail`. Questions and answers allow line
   feeds and tabs, no other control character; reasons are one line.
9. **Notifications.** Party events tell the other party as before (the organisation's people are its named contact
   and the members who acted on the engagement); system events tell both. An engagement nobody at the organisation
   has touched (a SUBMITTED expiry) tells only the developer: N01's "org admin" needs the role-seat dispatch of
   REQ-NOT-03 (later). Emails use the ADR-004 status-change layout (`notifications/status.py`,
   `templates/status.{txt,html}.j2`, the second extending `simple.html.j2` with the engagement ref), kinds
   `engagement.n01|n03|n05|n17|n20`, mutable per kind, once per event and recipient (`status:<event>:<user>`).

10. **Caps (review round 1).** policy.yaml `info_requested.info_requests_per_stage` (2 questions each time the
    engagement enters stage 1 or 2; a withdrawn question counts) and `on_hold.hold_days_total` (60 calendar days
    over all of an engagement's holds; a hold counts until it resumes, the current one until its resume date): 409
    `info_request_limit` and `hold_limit`, the actions hidden once spent (`Facts.questions_left`,
    `Facts.hold_days_left`, from the chain).
11. **The stuck question.** A question's deadline is its answer-by date (`info_requested.expire_bd`, 10 BD; the
    stage's own clock stays paused, its deadline in the payload); past it the expiry job ends the engagement
    `EXPIRED (NO_DEV_RESPONSE)` (the developer's silence; the end-reason CHECK allows no other code), told under
    N03, the tag expired. The organisation's `cancel_request` returns to the stage with the days it waited added
    like an answer's and writes **no note**: the notes' kinds stay four (no schema change; a cancel is not an answer
    and has no text); the History shows the `cancel_request` command and the developer is told by N03.
12. **The contact gate in notes (THREAT_MODEL I).** While the stage, or the stage a side state returns to, is before
    CONTACT_MADE, a question, an answer or a reason carrying an email, a phone number, a link or a payment number
    (`proposals.sanitise` detectors, raw and as plain text) is 422 `contains_contact`.
13. **A hold at stage 3.** The detail's `contact.contact_by` shows the contact-by date moved by the hold (the stage's
    deadline, while the stage entered with the named date); the row keeps the named date EM2 quoted (the job,
    bound to the developer, cannot write the organisation's contact columns).

### Demo

No seed change. `python -m bridge.demo clock` (`make demo-clock`) runs one pass of the expiry job after moving the
clock, so `make demo-clock DAYS=30` expires P3's SUBMITTED engagement with Telco A (20 BD, `NO_REVIEW`) and P4's
INTEREST_CONFIRMED engagement with SACCO B (10 BD, `CONTACT_NOT_MADE`) at once; the worker tells both parties. The
walkthrough moves the clock one day only, so none of its beats change, but its `beforeAll` requires P3 to be
SUBMITTED: run `make demo-reset` after demoing an expiry (README and `docs/demo/README.md` say so). `make demo-clock
DAYS=3` expires nothing.
