## Decisions taken (F-A, impl-frontend, 2026-10-02)

1. **The buttons stay in the actions card.** The banner says the side state and the step it waits on ("Your next
   step: Answer the question"); the developer's **Answer the question** (the one `[data-primary]`, from `awaiting`) and
   the organisation's **Withdraw the question** are the actions card's buttons, never repeated in the banner: one place
   for each party's buttons, from the API's `actions` only (docs/spec/06 6.9).
2. **Sheets.** The five commands open a native modal `<dialog>` drawn with ConfirmDialog's frame (`sheetClass`,
   `SheetHandle`, now exported; a bottom sheet on phones, the rise off under reduced motion), lazy-loaded
   (`SideSheet.tsx`, its values in `sheet.ts`). Focus goes to the first field (to Cancel for the withdrawal); the send
   button is styled, not marked, primary; a refused text stays open with the refusal (422, `hold_limit`), every other
   refusal closes the sheet and refreshes. The withdrawal is a confirmation in the primary tone (it ends nothing).
3. **Headlines and dates.** A hold reads "This engagement is on hold." with the ⏸ mark (nobody's turn); an open
   question keeps "Awaiting: …" (the developer's turn). The question's answer-by date is said once, in its sentence
   with the consequence ("Unanswered by …, the engagement expires."); past it the overdue countdown and the ⚠ chip
   show; a hold's date lives in its sentence only. An expiry is a whole sentence per reason (`tracker.expired.*`).
4. **"Question answered on {date}: {answer}"** shows while the stage the answer resumed lasts (the note's time at or
   after `stage_entered_at`, 2 s tolerance). A withdrawn question and an early resume get no banner (the resume's
   reason is on the History).
5. **Notes on the History** pair with events by order within their kind (`NoteOut` has no `seq`): the n-th
   `request_info`, `answer_info`, `pause` or party `resume` event gets the n-th note of its kind; the system's resume
   has none. If the API adds the event's `seq` to `NoteOut`, pair on it instead.
6. **Checks.** The sheets check only that the text (and a hold's date) is there; the date range and the days left
   are the API's (in dev and test the app's clock may be ahead of the browser's). The API does not send
   `hold_days_left` or `questions_left`, so the sheets state the rules (60 days a hold, 60 in all; two questions a
   stage) and word the refusals; showing what is left needs those on `EngagementDetail` (backend follow-up).
7. **Copy.** `refusal.containsContact` is the orchestrator's sentence verbatim; the numbers in the copy (60 days,
   two questions, ten business days) and the text limits are pinned to `policy.yaml` and `state_machine.py` by
   `state-machine-parity.test.ts`, which also checks every `Command` has a route and `PAUSED_STATES` = `RETURNING`.
8. **JS budget.** The tracker's first load grows by about 500 bytes gzipped (design-lab tracker, production build:
   157,123 → 157,621 with the lab's own scripts). The tracker measured 149,321 at P18; re-measure
   `/dev/engagements/<id>` after the merge (P19-C's bell is new since).
9. **The e2e clock scenarios** (`e2e/tracker-branches.spec.ts`, "on the test clock") move the shared clock for the
   whole database, so they run only with `E2E_TEST_CLOCK=1`, alone after the suite. The expiry pass needs no backend
   hook: the spec defers `engagements.expire` at once through `procrastinate_defer_jobs_v1` as the owner. Suggested CI
   step after `make check-e2e`: `E2E_TEST_CLOCK=1 npx playwright test tracker-branches -g "test clock" --workers=1`.
10. **Reads.** `stageLeft` traces an expiry through a side state to its stage; the screen reads no history for a
    paused engagement (`paused_from` names its stage). The design lab gains `?variant=question|question-org|hold|answered`.
