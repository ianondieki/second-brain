## Why these four

| Track | REQ-ID | What a person sees | Why now |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Tracker side states | REQ-ENG-10 (part), AC-PROP-3, AC-TRACK-4 (part) | An organisation can **Request information** (the clock pauses until the developer answers); either side can **Pause** an engagement before the agreement with a resume date; engagements nobody acts on **expire** on the test clock with the spec's reasons; each shows as a banner on the stage it happened at | The tracker is the product's spine; today it only knows the happy path plus Decline and Withdraw |
| B Problem Briefs | REQ-DIR-05 | A verified organisation **posts a brief** (title, statement, niche, county, budget band, deadline) from a new **Problems** section; staff moderate it like a developer-reported problem; developers find it on Discover under **Briefs** and start a proposal from it, already linked to the organisation | Closes the loop in the other direction: demand speaks, not only scouts |
| C In-app notifications | spec 07 §1 (the bell), REQ-NOT-03 (in-app channel) | A **bell** in the top bar with the unread count and a **Notifications** page; approvals, declines, interest, requests for information, pauses and expiries land there as they happen | Every event the tracker already writes in-app is invisible today; A's new events need a home that is not only email |
| D Editor trust checks | REQ-PROP-04, REQ-REPO-01 (over-disclosure, warn only) | Before publishing, a developer can **check overlap** with other owners' published teasers (a coarse band, never a score, never their text) and sees a **"this reads like how, not what"** warning when the teaser leaks approach detail | The honest-watermark story: evidence, controlled disclosure, no false promises |

Left for the next phase, recorded here on purpose: `DISPUTED`, `TERMINATED`, `PROCUREMENT_ROUTE`, `ON_HOLD` after
the agreement (needs the acknowledgement-or-dispute branch), invited-only briefs (the schema has `brief_invitations`;
the form offers public only and says so), messages (REQ-ENG-11), the responsiveness score's data source (the rules
and the cards are wired; the demo cannot show it honestly without 10 real tags and 60 days, see the P19 report).
